Organisation Overview

Runs low-cost refill dispensers for shampoo and household products in spaza shops and low-income retail markets, replacing single-use sachets. Developed in partnership with Unilever, Transform, Bopinc and DY/DX, with country-specific programmes in South African townships, Kenyan retail, and Bangladeshi low-income markets.

Business Model

Low-cost refill dispenser hardware for liquid and dry FMCG products (detergents, rice, sugar, coffee, snacks) targeting informal retail (spaza shops) to replace single-use sachets; dispensers weigh product and print a barcode automatically. In March 2026, Smartfill and DY|DX open-sourced the core dispenser design/technical architecture specifically to reduce hardware costs (target: under $50/dispenser, some product lines under $35) and accelerate refill adoption across the Global South — source: smartfill.store (2024); bizcommunity.com; packagingeurope.com (Mar 2026)

Customers

Informal-trade ('spaza shop') retailers and low-income consumers in South African townships (e.g. Tembisa), plus pilot markets in Zambia (Mtendere), Bangladesh, and Kenya; also piloted with formal retail (SPAR South Africa) and UK Research and Innovation (UKRI, London) — source: smartfill.store (Jun 2024); engineeringnews.co.za

Partnerships

Unilever (Kenya pilot dispensing Sunlight washing powder; also a Transform programme partner); SPAR South Africa (rice, sugar, coffee granules, coffee creamer pilot); UK Research and Innovation/UKRI (London pilot, chocolate-covered almonds/cashews/muesli); Bopinc; EY; UK Foreign, Commonwealth & Development Office — source: smartfill.store (Jun 2024); engineeringnews.co.za; bizcommunity.com

Plans (as of Apr 2025 grant announcement) to establish 14 refill-enabled stores and a low-emission distribution network in Tembisa (South Africa) and Mtendere (Zambia); no independently audited plastic-avoidance or sales-volume figures were found in this pass — source: engineeringnews.co.za (Apr 2025)
