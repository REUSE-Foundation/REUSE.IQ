Organisation Overview

Home pickup service for hard-to-recycle materials that also offers a B2B reusable-mailer pickup/return service, helping other circular-packaging businesses collect and redistribute mailers back to client warehouses.

Business Model

IMPORTANT CORRECTION vs. my original candidate entry: Ridwell is fundamentally a CONSUMER/HOUSEHOLD subscription pickup service for hard-to-recycle materials (plastic film, batteries, light bulbs, clothing/textiles, Styrofoam, corks, cords/chargers, eyeglasses, plastic bottle caps, multi-layer plastics), not primarily a B2B reusable-mailer logistics service as previously described. Drivers collect sorted materials biweekly from subscriber homes and route them to 200+ local/national recycling and reuse partners (e.g. plastic film becomes Trex composite decking; old clothing/blankets go to animal shelters as bedding; Styrofoam is reused in picture frames or packing peanuts). Company states approximately 50% of collections go toward REUSE via nonprofit partners rather than material recycling, addressing a genuine reuse-adjacent niche. A newer 'Ridwell Express' mail-back program extends the service nationwide beyond the doorstep-pickup markets — source: packagingdigest.com (2025); magazine.northwestern.edu (2024); pccmarkets.com (2024)

Customers

Households in Seattle, Portland, Denver, Minneapolis, and other US markets paying a monthly subscription ($12-16/month depending on market) for biweekly doorstep pickup of hard-to-recycle items — source: tcbmag.com (2021); ingoodtastedenver.com (2022)

Partnerships

200+ local and national recycling/reuse partners, including Trex (plastic film to composite decking) and various animal shelters (textile reuse); company publishes partner names and contamination-rate audits transparently on its own site — source: magazine.northwestern.edu (2024); pccmarkets.com (2024)

Circular Economy Activities

Reverse logistics / Reusable packaging (partial — mixed recycling and reuse model)

Measurable Impact

Since 2018, Ridwell has helped divert more than 21 million pounds of plastic and other hard-to-recycle materials from the waste stream (as of a Spring 2024 figure); an earlier 2021 figure cited over 2 million pounds diverted in Seattle alone, including 330,000+ lbs of clothing/textiles and 325,000+ lbs of plastic film since the start of COVID-19 — source: magazine.northwestern.edu (2024); geekwire.com (2021)
