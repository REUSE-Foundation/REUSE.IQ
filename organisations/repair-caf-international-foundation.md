# Repair Café (International Foundation)

Global nonprofit foundation supporting a worldwide network of volunteer-run 'Repair Café' events where people bring broken household items (electronics, clothing, furniture, toys) to be fixed for free by volunteer repairers, extending product lifespans and building repair skills in local communities.

## Quick Facts

- **Official name:** Stichting Repair Café / Repair Café International Foundation (RCIF) — source: repaircafe.org/en/foundation; en.wikipedia.org (Repair Café)
- **Also known as:** Repair Café Foundation — source: p2pfoundation.net wiki
- **Country / HQ:** Netherlands (Amsterdam; global network)
- **Website:** https://www.repaircafe.org — source: direct
- **Year founded:** First Repair Café event held 18 October 2009 in Amsterdam (Fijnhout Theater); the Repair Café Foundation/RCIF was formally established 2 March 2010 (some sources round this to '2011') — source: en.wikipedia.org (Repair Café); repaircafe.org/en/foundation
- **Est. company size:** Small core staff: day-to-day management by founder Martine Postma, plus employees Gertrud Maes, Claudia Schipper and Yasmine Stoop, and consultants Martijn Leusink and Tim Stok — source: repaircafe.org/en/foundation
- **Ownership status:** Non-profit foundation (Stichting), founded by Martine Postma; early funding included a grant of over $260,000 from the Dutch DOEN Foundation's social cohesion programme — source: en.wikipedia.org (Martine Postma)
- **Geographic scope:** Global — network reported at 1,000+ Repair Cafés in 2016, 2,000+ in 30+ countries by 2019, and 'almost 3,000' in 40+ countries by 2023 — source: en.wikipedia.org (Repair Café); ifixit.com; fixingthefuture.atlasofthefuture.org
- **Categories:** Repair
- **REUSE priority rating:** ★★★★★ Essential
- **SDGs:** SDG 12: Responsible Consumption and Production

## Organisation Overview

Global nonprofit foundation supporting a worldwide network of volunteer-run 'Repair Café' events where people bring broken household items (electronics, clothing, furniture, toys) to be fixed for free by volunteer repairers, extending product lifespans and building repair skills in local communities.

## Business Model

Global nonprofit foundation supporting a worldwide network of independently-run, volunteer-staffed 'Repair Café' events where the public brings broken household items (electronics, clothing, furniture, appliances) for free repair by volunteer experts, extending product lifespans; the Foundation provides a manual, starter kit, and (via its 'Repair Monitor' tool) data collection on repairability by product/brand — source: repaircafe.org/en/about; ifixit.com (2025)

## Customers

Not applicable in a commercial sense — serves the general public in local communities worldwide via volunteer-run repair events; ~2,000-3,000 affiliated local Repair Café groups across 30-40+ countries depending on year cited — source: ifixit.com (2025); fixingthefuture.atlasofthefuture.org (2023)

## Partnerships

DOEN Foundation (early grant funder); Centre for Sustainable Design (CfSD) at University for the Creative Arts (UK), which conducted academic surveys of the network in 2014 and 2016 — source: p2pfoundation.net wiki

Repair

## Measurable Impact

2019: prevented approximately 420,000 kg of waste and an estimated 10 million kg of CO2, across 2,000+ Repair Cafés worldwide that year — source: ifixit.com (2025, citing Foundation's own figures)
